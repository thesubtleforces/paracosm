# Voynich manuscript (Beinecke MS 408): fact sheet

Checked 19 November 2026 by a research pass over the sources below. It backs the primer in the
report. Each item carries a tag:

- **OK**: read at the source named.
- **SEC**: secondary source or metadata only.
- **UNVERIFIED**: could not be confirmed.

Quotes marked [DT] come from YouTube auto-captions and may contain transcription errors.
Three items were verified separately and are not repeated here: Layfield & Davis 2026
(*Digital Medievalist* 19), Greshko 2025 (*Cryptologia*), and Davis's 2024 post on the
multispectral images.

## 1. Dating and materials

- **Radiocarbon (OK).** University of Arizona, 2009. "Four samples from different bifolios"
  (ff. 8, 26, 47, 68); "between 1404 and 1438 with 95% probability" [ZC, ZO]. Led by Greg Hodgins
  (https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/624074, Feb 2011). Announced in Vienna, 8 Dec 2009.
  Caveats [ZC]: "I am not aware of a published paper"; Zandbergen's own recalculation gives
  1404–1435; it dates the parchment only, and the press-conference claim that the ink was applied
  immediately "was incorrect".
- **Species (OK).** Calf, "identified in 2014 by a team at the University of York" by protein
  comparison [ZD]; reported in Zyats et al., "Physical Findings", in Clemens (ed.), Yale 2016,
  pp. 23–37 [ZO n.2, ZR]. Only samples were tested: "the ones that have been tested were all
  calfskin" [DT]. The cover is goat (Jesse Meyer, Nov 2014) [ZO].
- **Number of skins.** The wording "at least fourteen or fifteen entire calfskins" is Eamon
  Duffy's, *NYRB* 20 Apr 2017, reviewing the Clemens volume (paywalled; seen in a search snippet
  and in Wikipedia's footnote). Whether Zyats et al. state it: UNVERIFIED. No skin count in D25
  or DT.
- **McCrone (OK)** [MC] (J. Barabe; sampled 14–15 Jan 2009; report 1 Apr 2009): "All of the inks
  used for text or drawing were identified as iron gall inks"; text and drawings "most likely
  created contemporaneously"; page-number, quire-mark and f1r-alphabet inks "all different from
  the text/drawing ink and from one another"; "The blue paint is ground azurite"; green "optically
  consistent with copper resinate, and copper-chloride compounds consistent with atacamite";
  red-brown "a red ochre"; white "glair (eggwhite) and calcium carbonate is likely"; gum binder.
- **Size.** "225 x 160 mm" [BK]; "23.5 cm high by 16.2 cm wide by about 5 cm deep" [MC].
- **Leaves (OK).** "102 folios (originally probably 116 of which 14 are missing) ... 18 gatherings
  or quires (originally probably 20 ...)" [ZD]. Missing: 12, 59–64, 74, 91–92, 97–98, 109–110.
- **Quire marks** "almost certainly date from the 15th Century" [ZO]. **Foliation:** sources
  disagree. "s. xvi" [BK]; Manly 17th c., De Ricci 16th c., 15th "may also not be excluded" [ZSO];
  Davis: "foliated in the 17th century when it was in Prague" [DT].
- **Binding.** Three sewing supports; earlier wooden boards (insect holes), tanned leather [ZO];
  present limp cover "s. xviii-xix" [BK], added by the Collegium Romanum Jesuits "between 1824 and
  1870" [ZH]; 1960s repair (Lehmann-Haupt, 1967) [ZD, D25].

## 2. Provenance

- **1637 (OK).** First approach c. Oct 1637 via Theodor Moretus SJ (letter lost); Kircher's reply
  to Moretus 12 Mar 1639 [ZL].
- **Baresch to Kircher** (APUG 557 f.353): "Pragae Anno Domini 1639. 27 die Aprilis" [PN];
  Zandbergen says "21 April 1639" [ZL]. The two disagree on the day.
- Baresch died "between 1646 and 1662"; Marci inherited [ZH]. (BH's "Barschius (1585–1622)"
  conflicts.)
- **Marci to Kircher (OK).** 19 Aug 1665 [ZL]; "The correct reading 1665 is clear" [ZH n.2];
  clinched by Kinner's letter of 4 Jan 1666 [ZT]. Beinecke still prints "AD 1666 [or 1665?]" [BK]
  and "in 1666" [BH]. Text [BK]: "dictum librum fuisse Rudolphi Imperatoris, pro quo ipse latori
  qui librum attulisset 600 ducatos praesentarit, authorem uero ipsum putabat esse Rogerium
  Bacconem Anglum. ego judicium meum hic suspendo". Informant "D. Doctor Raphael" = Mnišovský
  (d. 1644): hearsay, over twenty years old [ZSH].
- **Tepenec ex libris (OK).** f1r, faded or erased, "only visible under UV illumination", with the
  number 19 [ZH]. Noble form, so "after 1608"; he died 1622. "The first positively identified
  owner" [ZH]. (BH names Rudolf II "first confirmed owner"; Wikipedia names Baresch.)
- **Guzy 2022 (OK)** [G22]: "Book Transactions of Emperor Rudolf II, 1576–1612: New Findings on
  the Earliest Ownership of the Voynich Manuscript", CEUR-WS vol. 3313. "126 transactions of books
  ... approximately 6900 journal entries"; 1599: "a small number of alchemical manuscripts bought
  for 600 florin from the Augsburg physician Carl Widemann" (= 500 thaler; "ain väßl mitt allerlai
  selzamen büchern"). A matching sum, not the manuscript by name: it "seems to present a way in
  which the Voynich manuscript could have got into the Emperor's collection". A florin is not a
  ducat. Fuller version: *Herold-Jahrbuch* NF 28 (2023) [ZR].
- **Jesuits (OK).** Kircher, then the Collegium Romanum library; hidden in 1873 under labels
  "Ex Bibliotheca privata P. Petri Beckx" [ZH].
- **Villa Mondragone: wrong per current research.** "we now know that this is not true, and they
  were hidden in the Villa Torlonia in Castel Gandolfo" (Potenza 2022) [ZH, ZM]. BH still says
  "Jesuit College at Frascati".
- **Voynich (OK).** 1911 or 1912, via Fr Joseph Strickland SJ, under secrecy; price unknown
  [ZH, ZM].
- **1930–1969 (OK)** [K22]: the will left "60 percent ... to Ethel and 40 percent to Anne Nill";
  "On July 27, 1960, Ethel Voynich died"; Nill "sold the manuscript to H.P. Kraus for $24,500"
  (12 Jul 1961 [ZH]); "On September 24, 1961, Anne Nill died"; Kraus "initially asked $100,000 ...
  increased the price to $160,000"; "In 1969 he abruptly donated it to the Beinecke Library".

## 4. Internal evidence for place and milieu

- **Month names.** A later hand, "certainly a Romance language"; Zandbergen: "would appear to be
  Northern French" [ZW]. Occitan or Catalan: S. B. Palmer and N. Pelling, 2004
  (http://inamidst.com/voynich/months). Panofsky "provincial French" [ZSS]; Davis "a French
  dialect or maybe Catalan" [DT]. No consensus.
- **f116v (OK)** [ZQ]: first word "widely considered to say 'poxleber'"; last line "so nim geis
  mi(l)ch" (Salomon, 1930s; Panofsky); two Voynichese words; Gothic hand of the "first half of the
  15th century" (Ponzi, Gheuens).
- **f17r (OK).** "no credible reading ... has been proposed"; Voynichese under UV (Pelling) [ZW];
  Davis: "same hand as ... 116 verso", and it predates the water stain [DT].
- **Colour notes (OK)** [ZW, ZO]: "rot" on f4r (probably f7r), "g" under green on f1v; all in
  Hand 1; a parallel in Vicenza MS 362; suggests a German-speaking scribe.
- **Merlons (OK).** The swallowtail style is "found predominantly in what is now Northern Italy";
  "In German manuscripts only straight ... crenellations" [ZO]. Koen Gheuens's pre-1450 map
  (https://herculeaf.wordpress.com/2021/09/21/a-map-of-swallowtail-merlons/) comes with his own
  caution against "overly strong conclusions".
- **Crossbow Sagittarius.** "German MSs ... between (roughly) 1400 and 1500"; closest "several MSs
  from Southern Germany" (Ponzi, Worley, J. K. Petersen) [ZI]. Gheuens "and others": Alsace
  workshops, "most notably ... Diebold Lauber"; but, for example, the McKell calendar is "between
  1430 and 1450". The often-quoted "Lake Constance, c. 1400–1430" is UNVERIFIED as stated.
- **Clothing (OK).** Gheuens 2018: confirms the radiocarbon range "with a preference for the
  earlier half"; "venturing outside of the 1400-1430 range has consequences"
  (https://herculeaf.wordpress.com/2018/11/13/the-golden-age-of-baggy-elbowed-tunics/).
- **Panofsky (OK)** [ZSS]: 1931–32 "most probably in Spain", "about 1410-20-30"; 1954 "My guess is
  that the manuscript was produced in Germany", c. 1470; the Bacon theory is "at variance with all
  the available facts".
- **Toresella 1995.** Northern Italy, a humanist hand, "around 1460" (later than the radiocarbon
  range); a quack's showpiece [ZO, ZS].
- **Baths (OK).** Resemblance to manuscripts of *De Balneis Puteolanis* (B. Smith, A. McLean;
  Vat. Ross. 379) [ZI].

## 5. State of play, 2025–2026

1. **Layfield & Davis, DHQ 20(1), May 2026 (OK)** [LSA]: nearby chunks are more similar than
   distant or shuffled ones, so the manuscript "seems to follow a coherence pattern similar to
   known texts"; mean similarity 0.483, across page breaks 0.421, across section breaks 0.323,
   across A/B transitions 0.206 against 0.332 for B/B, "a substantive and significant difference
   between Language A and Language B"; the Biological, Pharmaceutical and Stars sections are
   cohesive; the results "allow us to speculate that there may indeed be semantic content"; the
   pattern is "odd" for a Timm–Schinner generator.
2. **Davis, "Voynich Codicology", Jan 2025 (OK)** [D25]: scribes alternate by bifolium, hence
   misbound; the foliation "appears to date from the seventeenth century"; the water stain (first
   seven quires) predates the misordering and the foliation; Quire 9 was "reoriented" (sewing
   holes at 67v2/67v1) "AFTER the quiremarks ... BEFORE the foliation". Her Toronto talk [DT]
   revises this: "Three stations sewn on double cords"; with "evidence of only one binding before
   the present covers, we can conclude that the initial binding was itself the misbinding"; the
   stain "occurred before the manuscript was bound"; originally unbound "singulions". Dissent:
   Zandbergen on Quire 9, "only tiny cuts ... never used in any previous binding" [ZSO]; Pelling,
   "wobbly foundation" [CM].
3. **Other.** Yale XRF of f1 (unpublished; DT): Marci's column appears "on the zinc map";
   Voynich's reagent was "liver of sulfur". *Cryptologia*, metadata only, not read: Ponnaluri,
   "The Voynich Manuscript was written in a single, natural language", 49(6), 2025
   (doi 10.1080/01611194.2024.2414128); Kinnison, "positional entropy collapse", 2026
   (doi 10.1080/01611194.2026.2697318); a "Technical rebuttal" to Naibbe
   (doi 10.1080/01611194.2026.2716121). Preprint, not peer-reviewed: Zhang & Sheng,
   arXiv:2610.34499, in which Timm–Schinner copying "predicts these statistics better than any
   cipher with one key". No new radiocarbon, protein or DNA tests found. An online conference is
   announced for 9 Dec 2026 (https://www.themedievalacademyblog.org/call-for-papers-the-voynich-manuscript/).
4. **Consensus statements (OK).** Zandbergen: "the MS was almost certainly created in the early
   15th Century ... at the very best the location where the MS was created remains speculation"
   [ZH]; "The Voynich MS may very well have no meaningful content, either deliberately by the
   author, or because its meaning was lost in the process of its creation" [ZS]. Davis: "I have
   found no compelling evidence indicating that the manuscript is NOT an authentic 15th-century
   object" [D25]; "the jury is still out on whether the text represents an actual human language,
   or a code, or a cipher, or nonsense" [DT]. Beinecke: "Cryptographic approaches have failed to
   decipher the text, if it is indeed encoded" [BH].

## Common misconceptions

- Found at Villa Mondragone (it was Castel Gandolfo); Marci's letter dated "1666"; John Dee as
  seller or foliator ("none of the three points can be substantiated" [ZSH]). All three are still
  on Beinecke pages, which also date the book to the "end of the fifteenth or during the
  sixteenth century" [BH].
- The radiocarbon result dates the writing. It dates the parchment only.
- Rudolf's 600 ducats as documented fact. It is hearsay; the archive shows 600 florins for
  several books.
- "Rugg 2003". Published 2004.
- "14–15 skins per Zyats". Traceable only to Duffy's review.
- Sunflower and other New World plants: "hardly believed anymore" [ZO].
- Zodiac parallels are mostly later than the radiocarbon window.
